# A focused path to the first clients

This is a proposed operating plan, not a revenue forecast. Keep the current storefront prices until the owner decides otherwise.

## Sell one useful outcome first

Lead with the $499 one-page site for one local service niche: a clear service list, proof from the business, and a prominent inquiry action. Pick a niche the owner can speak to credibly. Keep apps and dapps visible as expansion options rather than making every lead choose between unrelated technologies.

At the listed starting price, two $499 projects produce $998 gross project revenue before time, fees, taxes, and expenses. A $99 care plan is recurring service work, not passive income; define the monthly work limit and exclusions before selling it.

## A small weekly pipeline

- Select ten relevant businesses manually. Look for one specific, visible website issue you can explain respectfully.
- Build one private concept relevant to that niche, explicitly labeled as a concept. Don't imply an endorsement or copy a business's assets without permission.
- Prepare a short personal introduction with the observed issue, the useful improvement, and a link to Kshot's demo. The owner reviews and sends outreach; this plan authorizes no messages.
- Offer a concrete scope: one page, supplied content, agreed revision rounds, and a clear handoff. Quote integrations separately.
- Track prospects, replies, scoped proposals, accepted work, delivery time, and actual margin. Improve the offer based on evidence, not more speculative apps.

## Proposal essentials

Describe the customer's problem, intended audience, deliverables, content responsibilities, revision limit, external costs, delivery dependencies, handoff, payment milestones, and support scope. Agree these in writing before building. Have contract terms reviewed as needed; this is an operational checklist, not a legal template.

## Proof that helps the next sale

Ask completed customers for permission to publish a case study. Show the original problem, screenshots, what changed, and verified outcomes. Do not invent testimonials, client logos, conversion lifts, usage metrics, or “most popular” badges.

For the website, add measurement only when the owner chooses a privacy and analytics approach. Until then, manually count actual email inquiries and which package they mention. The brief builder intentionally stores and transmits nothing on its own.
